## The artistic change

The next film should make the audience feel that a person chooses to move, another person understands, and the world changes because of that exchange. Mother's Words of Love supply the dramatic meaning. The dancers, silk, light and camera should express the same event.

Keep the promo's warm gold people, mirror water, distant architecture, clear expression colors and journeys through depth. Give the performers enough darkness around their silhouettes for the audience to read a lifted heel, a soft wrist or a glance. Use the campaign's exact wording and colors from `engine/p3d/words.py`; any new expression assignment to a song needs deliberate integration with that song's existing plan.

The strongest contrast can be between intimacy and scale. Begin with a small act that is easy to understand. Let a sleeve or silk arc reveal its path. Then allow that path to continue into the water, a garden or the sky. The large event has emotional cause. A burst triggered by every beat would weaken that relationship.

## First proposed proof: making room for someone

Create a sixteen-second continuous scene with three ordinary women made of light. This is a proposed welcoming action, not a portrayal of Heavenly Mother and not a final song assignment. The movement should begin immediately with attention and a weight shift, avoiding the current study's long preparation.

Use one lead fan with an attached silk panel for the initial proof. Keep the fan open and in the same hand throughout. This makes its grip and attachment reviewable. The other two performers have free hands and distinct phrases. More fans and longer garments can follow once the first fabric feature works.

## What will make the movement beautiful

**Weight must lead the decorative movement.** A travelling arm phrase needs a supporting leg, a pelvis that moves over that support and a torso that participates. Turns should include a visible preparation and deceleration. A held foot must have a plausible reason to stay raised; the pose cannot simply wait for the end of a clock.

**The joints need purposeful sequence.** A shoulder may initiate an arc, the elbow carry it, and the wrist and fingers finish it. That order changes when the hand leads an invitation or a fan reverses direction. Applying the same delay curve to every gesture would make the performance feel mechanical.

**Attention gives the gesture an audience.** Heads and eyes should indicate the person, hand or destination being addressed. A response needs a brief recognition before it begins. The current rig has fingers and head controls; the work is to author their specific performance and prevent generic motion from disrupting a held grip.

**Fabric should carry the movement beyond the body.** The fan's grip is rigidly attached to the solved hand. Its silk has a defined attachment, width, length and rest shape. The free fabric lags, folds and settles. Keep any luminous history trail as a separate effect with a different visual treatment; it cannot stand in for cloth behavior.

**Variation needs structure.** Use unison at a chosen arrival, a canon for an intentional spreading response, and different phrases when people have different roles. The existing same-clip offset is useful for one of those relationships. It cannot supply all three.

## Model and costume references that help

The highest-priority reference is a clear moving full-body passage, with hands and feet visible. A second angle helps resolve turns and hidden limbs. A short close view of the signature hand gesture is more useful than a large collection of unrelated poses.

For body design, collect front, side and back silhouettes, intended height and proportions, and the amount of facial detail appropriate to the luminous style. Photorealistic faces are unnecessary. Readable neck direction, shoulder line, palms and posture can carry the emotion.

For the fan, record its open shape, handle, grip orientation, rib layout and exact silk attachment. For garments, show sleeve openings, skirt volume, hem length and how the fabric behaves during a turn and stop. Separate a robe sleeve, a long water sleeve and a hand-held silk panel as different constructions. A static outline does not determine their dynamics.

The first proof needs one reviewed hero body and costume configuration per role, one fan with a verified grip, one working silk feature and collision shapes matching the actual bodies. Avoid adding hair, extra veils and layered long sleeves simultaneously: each additional moving surface can conceal which feature needs correction.

## Growing into theatrical scale

After the trio works, build a small ensemble whose distinct roles can pass an action across the stage. A line can open into a crescent, two groups can make a corridor, and a foreground response can lead the eye toward a distant gathering. These are original staging options, not claims about the Cirque trailer.

Give each major transition a continuous, readable passage before designing closer camera coverage. Background people can use existing crowd methods. Foreground performers need explicit paths, spacing, attention and phrase changes. More bodies should deepen the event, not repeat the same person across the frame.

Aerial work is a separate production step. Choose a specific action after reviewing the reference, then model the actual apparatus or make a clearly intentional supernatural visual. For a physically grounded lift, author support, grip, load transfer, flight and landing together. Review both partners and the costume through the entire exchange. A person moving along an elegant curve does not by itself demonstrate a believable lift.
