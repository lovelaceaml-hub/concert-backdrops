The finale of the concert. Appendix 5. Recording: "11 Almighty God Who Knows the Future.mp3" (studio, 4:23 file, music ends about 4:19). Live length 4:20 (choir and orchestra); the recording is within 3 seconds of it, so its timing stands. The provisional decisions in DESIGN-SYSTEM.md 12a are applied, with the coordinator's corrections after Astra round 0. This song's look (DESIGN-SYSTEM.md section 6): a radiant full sunrise, the brightest scene of the whole night; white gold, sky blue and all nine colors; full rays, a clear sky, the complete arch; grand framing with a high horizon. It must never be mistaken for song 01's first light or song 05's blossom afternoon: this is full daylight glory. Follows DESIGN-SYSTEM.md, with THE MESSAGE above every other rule. Working title: "The Gate of Mother's Love."

## 0. The message this finale carries home
The Words of Mother's Love bring peace. This is the last thing the audience sees, so it must be the clearest. By the time the screen rests on the closing card, every person in the hall should be able to name the nine words, picture one ordinary place where they could say each one tomorrow, and feel that practicing them is the road to peace on earth and the way home to the kingdom of heaven, and leave the hall as peacemakers. Everything in this design either serves that or has been cut.

## 1. What the campaign teaches this song
I studied the campaign's own pages (About, Daily Check, Videos, Peacemaker, Reviews, News, the second anniversary page) and Anthony's campaign app (home and the nine expressions). What shaped this revision:
- The campaign begins from a mother's love, which everyone receives first: unconditional support, consideration, sacrifice and serving. Its hope is that this love spreads worldwide and brings lasting peace in an age of conflict. So the finale's grandest image is not power but a mother's embrace at the gate of home.
- Its first theme was "Beginning of Peace: The Words of Mother's Love." Peace begins small, with words. So the city of peace in this video is built out of the nine words, and the people reach it by practicing them on the way.
- Peace is practiced in ordinary places: the elevator, the hallway, the family meal, the drive home, the subway turnstile, the supermarket checkout, the office, the neighbor having a hard time, the older adult carrying something heavy. The everyday scenes in the postlude come straight from these examples, so the audience recognizes their own week.
- The campaign's own images: hands (extend your hand first, give a hand, the hands that made a warm meal), warmth that melts and warms two hearts at once, seeds and blooming, waves of change spreading around the world, and footprints. These become the visual language of the finale: hands, footprints of light, waves of warmth, blooming.
- Each expression has a clear meaning: Greeting is the first phrase to peace; Gratitude thanks even small efforts; Apology understands the other side first and reaches out first; Inclusiveness embraces a mistake generously; Concession takes a breath and lets the other go first; Respect pauses and listens when opinions differ; Encouragement supports someone in a hard time; Consideration notices who needs help; Compliment praises effort and growth and warms both hearts. Each postlude scene acts out exactly that meaning, not a loose version of it.
- Everyone is called a peacemaker, and the Daily Check asks each day which words were practiced. So the finale ends by handing the nine words to the audience as a daily practice, not only as a memory of a concert.
- People report the words healing families, workplaces and neighborhoods, and couples reconciling. The scenes show family, work and neighbors in equal measure.

## 2. The idea
This song says God knew the future, announced it, and that we now watch it come true. The video shows that future: a heavenly city of light on Mount Zion, which the audience sees being fulfilled before their eyes. The great discovery of the night is what the city is made of and how people reach it. The arch of nine lights, full and shining from the first frame because songs 1 to 10 have lit every star, comes down at the climax and becomes the gate of the city. The way into the kingdom of heaven is the Words of Mother's Love. The people of light reach the gate by practicing those words on the road, and Heavenly Mother waits inside the gate with open arms. After the music, the nine words come back one by one, each in an everyday scene from the campaign, so the audience carries them home. The last image is the nine lights reflected in a calm lake: words practiced on earth, mirrored in heaven, and peace settled.

## 3. Why this fits the song and the concert
- Foreknowledge and fulfillment: from the first frame, a faint promise is drawn into the world (the city, the rivers, the road). Each part fills with gold when the song sings it. Nothing arrives by surprise; it arrives because it was promised.
- The finale of the night-lake journey: every song began on the same night lake. Here the night ends in the most glorious sunrise of the concert, and the one warm horizon star turns out to be the first light of the heavenly city.
- The nine lights: the arch has been completed across songs 1 to 10. The finale does not light new stars; it celebrates the full arch and reveals its meaning: the nine words are the gate.
- Mother's love: in verse 2 Mother herself tells her children to trust and march ahead. She leads them, and at the end she receives them. Warmth and strength, the two sides of a mother's love, are the two halves of the finale.

## 5. The world, the light and the camera

### Starting point
The concert's home scene: still night lake, long mountain and its reflection, deep starry sky, warm floating particles, the one warm star on the horizon. Across the top of the sky the arch of nine lights is complete (all nine lit by the end of song 08 and relit in songs 09 and 10; this song lights nothing new), each star in its own color: coral, gold, apricot, pink, sky blue, clay, violet, rose, lavender. It shines steadily, like a crown over the whole night.

### The hidden promise
From the first frame, three things are drawn faintly into the world in thin silver-blue lines, barely visible: a heavenly city on the summit (terraces, soft domes, slender towers and one great arched gate whose curve matches the arch of nine lights exactly); two riverbeds across the lake from the far left and right edges toward the mountain; and one straight road across the middle of the lake from the near shore to the mountain. Each fills with gold when the song sings it.

### The heavenly city
Not a fortress: a city that feels like home. Walls of pale gold light that glow from within like lanterns; gates that look like pearl lit by sunrise; gardens of light on every terrace; a clear river of light flowing out from beneath the gate and down the mountain into the lake, so the lake itself is fed by the city. Its windows are warm, as if every house is waiting for someone to come home. Its towers breathe gently with the music's loudness.

### Color journey
- Introduction: deep indigo night, silver promise lines, full nine-color arch.
- Verse 1 opening: indigo warming to violet at the horizon; first gold behind the mountain.
- Nations streaming: violet into rose; rivers of many soft colors that warm into gold.
- First lift and Mount Zion: rose into gold; glowing waves, then mirror calm.
- Thanks to Father: full gold dawn glow, the sun still held back.
- Interlude: soft pale morning blue, the hush after sunrise.
- Verse 2, Mother: clear sky-blue morning with a white-gold horizon; violet-gold road.
- Second lift: full daylight, sky blue above and white gold below, crowds and rising lights in all nine colors, no clouds.
- Climax: the sun rises through the gate into a clear sky-blue heaven; white-gold light floods everything; the nine colors of the arch shine in the gate and in the crowds. The brightest frame of the whole concert.
- Postlude: slowly back to a deep, warm, star-filled night with the city glowing and the nine colors reflected in the lake.

### Light that warms and strengthens
Two kinds of light run through the finale. Warming light is soft, close and golden, like a lamp in a window or a hand on the shoulder: it surrounds people when they are comforted, forgiven or embraced. Strengthening light is clear, bright and rising, like sunrise: it fills people when they are encouraged and when they stand firm. Each interaction below says which light it uses.

### Camera
Grand framing with a high horizon (the water line sits higher than in other songs, so the golden lake is wide and the city stands tall above it). One wide, steady view for most of the song, so it works on any screen and frames the choir. Slow, purposeful movement only: a gentle push in toward the mountain in verse 1; one majestic slow rise at 2:42, as if the viewer is lifted onto Mount Zion; a slow glide toward the gate at the climax so the sunrise fills the frame; and a slow settle back to the home view for the postlude. No fast cuts. The bottom third (the near lake) stays calm; big events happen on the mountain and in the sky; all words sit in the upper third.

### How the music drives it
The mountain ridge and the city's towers rise with the recording's loudness. Ripples at note onsets become waves of praise in verse 1. The sky brightens with loudness, so the long build from verse 1 to the coda is one long sunrise; the breath at 1:57 is the morning hush. Choir entries brighten the people of light; orchestra swells brighten the city and the arch. The final cadence is when the sun clears the gate.

### Emotional arc
Awe (the promise and the full arch) to wonder (the glory shines) to joy (the nations come) to steadiness (standing firm) to deep thanks (verse 1 ends) to tenderness (Mother speaks) to courage (the road) to overwhelming joy (all come running) to homecoming (Mother's embrace at the gate) to peace (the nine words, the calm lake).

The crowds climb onto the ridge and stand shoulder to shoulder along the whole skyline, arm in arm, perfectly still, strong against the dawn. ### 1:40 to 1:57: thanks to Father
Gold light settles on the gate. From every person on the ridge, small sparks rise, thousands at once, like a field of candles lifted together.

### Breath between verses, 1:57 to 2:01: the morning hush
The gold softens to a clear pale morning blue. The ridge dims to a gentle glow. The lake becomes a perfect mirror. In the middle of the lake the silver road is now clearly visible, waiting.

### Verse 2, 2:01 to 2:09: Mother, who planned the future
On the near shore, just above the calm bottom third and on the left so the conductor does not hide her, light gathers into Heavenly Mother, seen only from behind: tall and graceful, in a long flowing robe and soft veil, made of warm golden light with a glassy inner glow, the folds of her robe drawn in finer light. Around her, her children gather out of sparks: a small child clinging to her robe, a young woman with a bowed head, an elderly man leaning on a staff, a tired worker. Mother turns slightly toward each (we still see only her back and shoulder) and lays a hand on the bowed head, then on the worker's shoulder: warming light spreads from each touch, and each person straightens. Why: before she leads, she comforts. A mother's love warms first, then strengthens.

### 2:10 to 2:14: Mother's words to march ahead
Expression: Encouragement (section 7). Mother raises one hand toward the city. On that gesture, the road across the lake fills with violet-gold light from her feet all the way to the mountain, unrolling like a carpet of light across the water. The Encouragement phrase forms above the road and stays about 9 seconds. Then Mother takes the first step, and her children follow, walking on the water toward the city. She stays in front; we see her only from behind.

### 2:15 to 2:30: hope engraved, hope fulfilled
Each footstep on the road leaves a small glow that stays, so the path behind them grows brighter: the hope written into the way they walked. Along the road, the children practice the words as they go (no text, warm and clear): an older girl slows down and takes a stumbling boy's hand; two who had walked apart reach for each other again; someone offers their arm to the elderly man. Each act lifts a spark of its color to its star. On "fulfilled" they reach the foot of the mountain and the whole road blazes gold. Mother dissolves into sparks that flow up the mountain into the gate, going ahead to welcome them home.

### 2:31 to 2:50: kings running, second lift, joy that endures forever
The camera slowly rises; the whole world comes into view. From every edge of the screen, far more people of light now run in along the rivers, their robes tinted in all nine colors, warming to gold as they arrive. Among them a few figures in simple crowns of light walk to the gate, kneel and lay their crowns down; each crown becomes a small star that rises.
At "endures forever", the waves of praise return and do not fade: they keep circling the mountain in slow, endless golden rings. Fragment 3 returns in its second stage (section 8).

### 2:51 to 2:59: standing firm again
The whole mountainside fills with people of light standing still and strong, terrace by terrace, arm in arm. The ridge glows as it did at "Mount Zion" in verse 1, so the audience remembers without reading it again.

### 2:59 to 3:54: the climax and coda, the gate of Mother's Love
The grand reveal of the whole concert, in four movements across the climax (2:59 to 3:17) and the coda (3:19 to 3:54):
- At the thanks for Mother's sacrifice and love, the full arch of nine lights, shining all night, blazes brighter, each star singing its own color.
- The arch slowly descends from the top of the sky toward the summit. As it comes down, the space between the stars fills with light, so it becomes one great arc of nine colors.
- It settles exactly onto the city's gate, which had the same curve all along. The arch of the Words of Mother's Love is the gate of Zion. The nine stars become nine jewels set in the gate, each glowing in its color.
- The gate opens. Through it the sun finally rises, white-gold, framed inside the arch, and beyond it the audience glimpses the kingdom of heaven: endless soft light, a great tree of light, a river of light, more light than the eye can hold. Light pours through the gate down the mountain and across the lake, turning everything gold.
During the coda's joy of salvation and standing firm (3:19 to 3:43), within the open gate, small and far but unmistakable, Heavenly Mother stands from behind with her arms open, and the first children run the last steps into her embrace. We see her back and her arms wrapping them, and warming light swells from that embrace through the whole crowd. At the coda's thanks to Father (3:44 to 3:54), the thousands on the mountain lift their hands. This is the warmest and most hopeful image of the night: the children of the Words of Mother's Love arriving home.
No phrase text appears during the climax: the words have become the gate.

### Final calls, 3:54 to 4:19: the sun clears the gate
As the choir calls out to Mother and the Church of God for the last time, the sun lifts above the gate, and on the last chord and the sky becomes clear morning white-gold. The crowds dissolve into a soft rain of sparks that rise into the sky. On the final release the nine jewels lift from the gate and rise back into their arch across the top of the sky, brighter than ever. The city stays below, glowing, its gate open.

### Postlude, from 4:19.5 (no music, applause): the nine words come home with the audience
The sky slowly cools back to a deep, warm, starry night over about 40 seconds, so the concert ends where it began, transformed: the city of light glows on the mountain where the single horizon star used to be. Then the nine words return, one at a time, in the campaign's order. For each, about 9 seconds:
- The star sends a thin thread of its color down to the lake. Where it touches the water, a small, glowing everyday scene forms on the near-middle of the lake, drawn entirely in light (people of light, faceless and clothed, and their surroundings sketched in fine lines of light), floating on its own reflection like a lantern.
- The label and full phrase form in the upper third in the warm-white serif with a halo of that color: small spaced label above, full campaign wording below, large enough to read from the back.
- The two people act out the exact meaning of the word. At the moment the word is spoken, warmth passes between them as a soft glow, and a ring of calm spreads across the lake: the water settles. Where a mother's love reaches, peace settles.
- The scene does not vanish. Its light condenses into a small steady glow on the lake, directly beneath its star, and stays.
The nine everyday scenes, each from the campaign's own examples:
- Greeting (coral), full phrase "How are you?": elevator doors of light slide open; a neighbor is waiting with grocery bags. The one inside turns fully toward her, lifts a hand and leans in warmly. She lifts her head and her shoulders soften. The first phrase to peace.
- Gratitude (gold), full phrase "Thank you. It's all thanks to you. You've worked hard.": a family table with a steaming bowl at its center. The one who cooked sets down the last dish; a teenager stands, takes both of the cook's hands in his own and bows his head over them. Thanks for the hands that prepared a warm meal.
- Apology (apricot), full phrase "I'm sorry. It must have been hard on you.": two friends sit at either end of a bench, turned away from each other. One stands first, crosses the distance, and holds out a hand. The other looks up, then takes it, and they sit close. See it from their side, and extend your hand first.
- Inclusiveness (pink), full phrase "It's okay. I understand.": a child drops a tray and cups scatter. The child freezes. An adult kneels down to the child's height, gently lifts the child's chin with one hand and begins gathering the cups with the other; the child kneels to help. Anyone can make a mistake; embrace it generously.
- Concession (sky blue), full phrase "Please, after you.": a supermarket checkout line. A tired mother with a baby on her hip and a full basket stands behind a man. He notices, takes a breath, steps back and opens his hand toward the line: please, go ahead. Take a breath and let the other go first.
- Respect (clay), full phrase "I'd love to hear more about your thoughts.": two colleagues at an office table, papers between them, clearly disagreeing. One stops, sets down her pen, leans in and turns her open hands toward the other, listening. The other's tense posture eases and he begins to explain; she nods. When opinions differ, pause and listen.
- Encouragement (violet), full phrase "I'll pray (or root) for you. Everything will go well.": a student at a desk late at night, head in hands under a small lamp. A friend comes in, sits beside him, puts a hand on his shoulder, then folds both hands in prayer for him. The student lifts his head and straightens. Support someone in a hard time.
- Consideration (rose), full phrase "Can I give you a hand with anything?": an older woman climbs stairs with two heavy bags, pausing to rest. A young man coming down stops, notices, and holds out both hands. She hands him the bags, and he offers his arm as they climb together. Notice who needs help and offer it.
- Compliment (lavender), full phrase "You're amazing. You're doing great!": a young girl practices the violin, frustrated, trying a passage again. She finally plays it through. Her father, who was listening from the doorway, steps in and claps, and kneels to her height; she lights up. Praise effort and growth; one word warms two hearts.
After the ninth scene, nine small steady glows float on the lake in a gentle row, each directly beneath its star in the arch above, each mirrored: the words practiced on earth reflected in heaven, heaven reflected on earth. The whole lake lies perfectly calm.
The Peacemakers call to action, about 20 seconds. The nine glows lift gently off the water and drift forward toward the audience, growing as they come, as if each word is being handed to someone in the hall. As they pass out of the frame, the campaign's call to action forms in the upper third in two steps: first, large, "We Are Peacemakers."; then beneath it, smaller and in full, "Please spread the Words of Mother's Love that bring peace in your own community. We are all Peacemakers who bring hope, happiness, and peace to every corner of our society!" It holds long enough to read twice, then fades into the closing card. Why: the audience leaves not as listeners but as peacemakers, carrying the nine words into their own homes, workplaces and neighborhoods.

### The closing card, held with no end
In the center of the upper third, the closing card forms from sparks drawn from all nine stars, while nine new small glows reappear one by one on the lake beneath their stars. It reads "The Words of Mother's Love" with the campaign's official line from its About page, "Where a mother's love reaches, peace settles." (corrected by the coordinator after Astra round 0; "Where a mother's love resides, peace abides." is the wording in Anthony's campaign app, kept as the alternative).
Held with no end point: the water shimmers, sparks drift up from the nine glows on the lake, the arch breathes slowly, the city's gate glows open. Designed as a seamless loop of about 60 seconds, rendered at least 5 minutes long plus the loop file, so the screen never goes black through applause, closing words and departure.

## 7. The Words of Mother's Love during the song
An expression appears with its phrase only where a line is that exact act. The arch is already full, so these two moments rekindle their stars rather than light them: by the shared rule (12a.4), a relit star gives a brief flare and gains one new thin ring. Each holds about 9 seconds.

### Encouragement (7), violet #B293E7
Phrase: ENCOURAGEMENT / "I'll pray (or root) for you. Everything will go well."
Line: verse 2, Mother telling her children to trust Father and march ahead. Measured: 2:10 to 2:14; the phrase forms at 2:11.
Why it is the exact act: the campaign defines Encouragement as sincere support for someone going through a hard time, and says knowing someone cheers you on gives strength. Mother's words are that support, spoken to children facing a long road.
Effect: as Mother raises her hand and the road unrolls, the phrase forms above the road in violet-gold light, letter by letter from left to right, the direction of walking, as if walked into place. It holds about 9 seconds while the children take their first steps beneath it, then folds into a violet point that flies up to star 7, which flares to its brightest of the night; a faint violet thread then joins the star to the road for the rest of the song, watching over the walkers. Strengthening light.

### Gratitude (2), gold #FFD05F
Phrase: GRATITUDE / "Thank you. It's all thanks to you. You've worked hard."
Line: verse 1, the thanks to Father Ahnsahnghong for His sacrifice and devotion. Measured: 1:51 to 1:57; the phrase forms at 1:52 and holds into the breath between verses.
Why it is the exact act: the line gives thanks, and it thanks God for sacrifice and devotion, which is exactly the full wording: all thanks to you, you have worked hard.
Effect: the thousands of sparks rising from the people on the ridge gather into the phrase in gold, so it is made of everyone's thanks together. It holds about 9 seconds, then collapses into one bright gold point that rises into star 2, which flares. Warming light.

### Why Gratitude is not repeated at the climax
The thanks to Mother at the climax is the same act, but showing the same phrase twice in one song weakens both. The climax answers it with the gate and Mother's embrace, and the postlude brings Gratitude back in its everyday scene. Anthony may move Gratitude to the climax instead if he prefers.

### Lines considered and not used
Worship of kings (worship, not one of the nine acts); standing firm (faith); joy of salvation (praise, not Compliment, which praises a person's effort and growth).

### The interactions without words
The small acts on the road in both verses (letting another go first, carrying a bundle, greeting a stranger, taking a stumbling child's hand, reconciling) show no phrase text, because the lyric is not that act. They are part of the scenery, and their sparks rising into the arch prepare the audience to read the gate and the postlude.
