# 01 All You Stars of Zion: Video Design

Concert: The Words of Mother's Love Healing Concert (theme: the Words of Mother's Love that bring peace), LV Zion, Sunday December 6, 2026, 4:30 PM, 6720 W Sahara Ave, Las Vegas.

Song: 01 All You Stars of Zion (songbook 121). Played live by a brass ensemble, about 3:08, with no singing. The studio recording in this folder (sung, 4:03.85) is the timing reference until a rehearsal recording exists; live, an operator advances each section and every section ends in a calm hold.

Role in the show: the opening song. It introduces the world (the night lake), the title style, the empty arch of nine lights, and the one message of the whole evening: the Words of Mother's Love bring peace.

Status: design proposal for Anthony's review. Version 3, November 13, 2026: continuity pass (provisional decisions of DESIGN-SYSTEM.md section 12a, star history, live length, hand-off) and a 1 minute promo rendered in the cloud. See the last two sections.

## The message at the center

The songs are the vehicle; the nine expressions are the point. In this first video the audience should leave knowing two of Mother's words (Compliment and Encouragement), what each looks like in everyday life, and that practicing them brings peace. Every scene, effect and figure below is chosen to make those two moments clearer, warmer and more memorable. Wherever one of Mother's words is spoken in this video, the same thing happens in the world: the water stills, the light warms, and peace settles.

## What the campaign teaches this song

I studied the campaign pages (About, Daily Check, Video, Peacemaker, Reviews, News, the second anniversary page) and the campaign app's nine expressions. What shaped this revision:

- The campaign's own sentence is the heart of the design: where a mother's love reaches, peace settles. So peace is not shown as fireworks but as settling: ripples smoothing into a mirror, wind dropping, light growing warm and still. Each expression in this song ends with the whole lake going perfectly calm around the people who practiced it.
- The words are small, everyday and a beginning. The campaign is called the Beginning of Peace, and it says peace starts with one short word to the person in front of you. That is why this opening song is the dawn of the concert: the first light of morning, starting from two small words between friends.
- A mother's love is the first love anyone receives, and it reaches across nations and cultures. The light in this video always comes first from Mother (seen only from behind, far on the shore) and then passes from person to person.
- Compliment, in the campaign's words, affirms effort and growth with heartfelt praise, and one kind word is enough to warm two hearts at once. So the Compliment moment shows exactly that: when one young person praises another's effort, both of their hearts glow at the same moment.
- Encouragement is sincere support for someone in a hard situation, reminding them they have your support; the campaign says knowing people cheer you on gives strength in any situation. So the Encouragement moment shows a tired young person who has fallen to their knees, a friend who kneels beside them, and strength visibly flowing into them as they rise.
- The campaign is carried in daily life by volunteers, many of them students and young adults (university, young worker and student volunteer groups), and 2026 is the UN International Volunteer Year in its plans. The youth of Zion in this song are therefore shown as young volunteers doing ordinary good work: cleaning the shore, carrying water for others, planting young trees.
- The Daily Check asks each person what kind of peace they achieved today. The nine seeds of the arch are that checklist in the sky: each word practiced fills one light.
- The campaign calls everyone a peacemaker. By the end of this song the young people of light are seen as peacemakers sent out at dawn, carrying light to every shore of the world, with the hope of the heavenly kingdom shining on the far horizon.

## 1. The idea: "From Starlight to Sunrise"

The song calls the youth of Zion stars, morning stars and dew of the dawn. So the video is one single night turning into one single, glorious morning. It opens on the home scene: a still lake under a deep sky of stars, and nine empty places for lights traced faintly across the heavens. As the brass plays, the stars of the sky come down to the shore and become young people made of light. They serve, they praise one another, they join hands, they lift torches, they help each other up. Every time one of Mother's words is practiced, the world answers: the night softens, the water stills, the sky warms. At the last chorus the sun rises out of the lake, the word "Arise" stands up off the water, and far beyond the mountains, in the gold of the sunrise, the faint radiant outline of a heavenly city appears: the hope the whole concert walks toward.

Two of the nine lights are lit by this song: Compliment (praising the faithful work of the young) and Encouragement (cheering them on to rise). The other seven seeds wait, glowing faintly, so the audience feels from the first minute that the evening is a journey and that every word of love will be needed.

Important for the live concert: live, the brass ensemble plays with no singers, so the audience will not hear the words. The words that appear on screen are the only lyrics the audience receives. That is why the expressions are the largest, longest and calmest moments of the video, and only three short lyric fragments appear besides them.

## 3. The look, the color journey and the camera

### The heavenly look

Everything is made of light and is luminous rather than flat: deep skies with layered nebula glow in soft violet and blue, stars with fine four-point glints, a lake like dark polished glass that mirrors every light, mountains with a faint rim of light on their ridges, warm golden particles drifting slowly upward like embers of prayer. Light is soft-edged and blooms gently. Nothing is harsh, dark or frightening. The feeling is sacred, warm and hopeful, like being held.

### Color journey (night to glorious morning)

- Intro and verse 1: deep midnight navy (about #0B1433) with a soft violet Milky Way, silver-white stars, one warm gold star resting on the horizon (the morning star). Water black and mirror still.
- End of verse 1: a thin plum and indigo glow rises along the horizon. The first gold of torches appears.
- Chorus 1: pre-dawn. Violet and dusty rose low in the sky, gold torch light along the shore. At 1:24 the whole sky flushes rose-gold for a few seconds, then settles.
- Verse 2: the blue hour. Cool teal and slate blue (about #1D3B5C), silver mist lying on the lake, silver dew, the green-gold glow of the tree of light.
- Chorus 2: dawn. Peach, apricot and gold climb the sky; at 3:22 the sun rises. The final minute is a radiant gold-white morning with a long golden road of sunlight on the water, and beyond the mountains the faint shining outline of the heavenly city.
- Hold after the music: calm early morning, soft gold and pale heavenly blue.
Every color step happens slowly (15 to 40 seconds), so nobody notices it until they realize the night has gone. Fast color moments happen only on three brass swells: 1:24, 2:07 and 3:10.

### Camera feel

Steady, wide and noble, like a slow crane gliding over the lake: never cuts, never shakes. Verse 1 is a slow push in toward the shore. The two expression moments are the only medium shots of people: the camera eases in gently so the audience can see the tender gesture clearly, holds still for the whole moment, then eases back out. Choruses add a gentle upward drift, as if we are rising with the light. Verse 2 opens on one close, shallow-focus shot (the dew), then pulls back. The last chorus lifts the camera until the horizon sits lower and the sky, the sunrise and the far heavenly city have room.

### How the music drives the picture

- Brass attacks: the nearest stars flare with a fine cross-shaped glint and one ring crosses the water (the engine's onset ripples).
- Loudness: drives the horizon glow and the skyline as in song 3, kept subtle, so the mountains breathe.
- Swells at 1:24, 2:07, 3:10 and 3:22 each have their own visual release.
- Quiet pockets at 0:13, 1:33, 2:12 and 2:40 are where the picture rests.
- During the two expression moments the music still moves the stars, but nothing new happens in the scene: no fragments, no new effects, nothing competing with the words.

### Screen layout

Arch of nine lights across the top of the sky (apex about 8 percent from the top, its ends about 30 percent from the top at the screen edges). Words appear in the upper third, under the arch. The people stand on the shore at the water line, which in song 01 is low (24 percent up from the bottom of the screen) to give the sky the most room. The bottom third is the lake: calm reflections only, because the brass ensemble stands in front of it.

## 4. Section by section, line by line

Lines are named by section and number with a few key words, so the lyrics are not copied here. The full lines are in the "Concert Backdrops - Lyrics" Google Doc.

### Before the music: preshow (proposal)

While the audience is seated, the home scene plays as a silent, slow loop: night lake, stars, the warm horizon star, faint golden particles rising. No title and no arch yet.

### Intro, 0:00 to 0:15 (measured): the world, the title and the empty arch

- 0:00 to 0:03: the starfield gently brightens and the horizon star glows warmer. Water perfectly still.
- 0:04, first brass hit: the title forms as a constellation. About forty stars flare at once and fine lines of light connect them like a star chart, bending into the elegant serif letters of All You Stars of Zion, large in the upper third, with stars twinkling at the corners of each letter. Beneath it the concert title, The Words of Mother's Love Healing Concert, writes itself in a row of tiny warm stars and holds a moment longer than the song title, so the first words of the evening are the message itself. Both reflect faintly in the lake.
- 0:10 to 0:11, second brass hit: the empty arch appears. A hair-thin silver line traces itself across the top of the sky like a bow being drawn, and nine small seeds of light appear along it, dim and colorless, each pulsing once very softly. Nine places waiting for nine words of love.
- 0:12 to 0:15, quiet pocket: the titles glide to their small resting place in the top left, under the left end of the arch. The sky holds its breath.

### Verse 1, 0:16 to 1:11

Line 1, about 0:16, "All you stars of Zion": the stars come down. Stars fall slowly and gracefully from the sky like a gentle snow of light, each with a soft trail. As each one reaches the shore it unfolds, like a flower opening, into a young man or young woman made of light, clothed in white-gold, standing on the shoreline. About twenty figures gather, small in the frame, their reflections in the mirror of the lake. Where the stars left the sky, the sky does not grow darker but softer and warmer, as if heaven gave its best to the earth.

Line 2, about 0:27, "like the morning star": the horizon star rises a little above the mountains and its rays lengthen into a fine golden cross, the brightest point in the sky. No words here; the picture says it. Its light falls across the water to the young people, and they turn toward it.

Line 3, about 0:38 to 0:48, "faithful and beautiful work": COMPLIMENT (section 5). The young people are serving as volunteers: lifting debris from the shore, carrying jars of water up from the lake for others, planting a row of young trees of light. The camera eases in on one young woman who has been working the longest: she sets down her jar, straightens slowly, tired. A young man beside her looks at the long clean shore and the row of planted trees behind her, sees how much she has done, and turns to her with an open hand. The words appear. Her heart glows, and in the same instant his heart glows too: one kind word warming two hearts at once. Then the calm: ripples on the lake smooth into a perfect mirror around them.

Line 4, about 0:49, "beautiful unity" and Mother's joy. The young people turn to each other and join hands along the whole shore. Where hands meet, a thread of light passes from one to the next until a single ribbon of gold runs the length of the shore, doubled in the lake. At the far right, on a gentle rise above the water, a tall robed figure of light is seen only from behind: Heavenly Mother, watching Her children. The ribbon of unity flows up to Her, and in answer a warm glow spreads outward from Her across the sky, the first plum and rose of morning: Her joy shown as warmth filling the heavens, never as a face. The build at 0:53 to 0:57 carries this glow; at 0:58 everything rests for one breath.

Line 5, about 1:00 to 1:11, "like a torch": fragment 1 (section 6). One young person raises a hand and a flame appears in it. The flame passes hand to hand down the line until every figure holds a torch, and the torches shimmer in the water, the first real gold on the lake.

### Chorus 1, 1:12 to 2:11

About 1:12, "O you stars of Zion": the young people raise their torches high and each flame flares into a small cross-shaped star, so the shoreline becomes a row of stars standing on the earth: the stars of Zion. The real stars above answer with a twinkle. The horizon turns violet and dusty rose.

About 1:24 to 1:31 (loudest), "Arise" and "the glory of our Mother": all torches lift together on the brass peak. From every torch a slender beam of gold rises; the beams lean toward the far right where Heavenly Mother stands and meet above Her in a vast, soft radiance, like a halo of morning behind the mountains. The whole sky flushes rose-gold. The nine seeds of the arch shimmer as the beams pass but stay waiting: the glory belongs to Her.

About 1:33 to 1:41 (soft pocket), "power and strength": the motion reverses and light comes down. From Mother's direction and from high above the arch, a warm, wide fall of golden light, like a gentle wind made of light, crosses the lake and settles into every torch and every heart. The flames steady and grow whiter, the figures stand straighter: the strengthening that comes from a mother's love. This is the only time in the song light moves downward, so the gift reads clearly. Never a figure or face of God, only light.

About 1:42 to 1:56, "Now let us arise": the young people step off the shore and walk out onto the mirror of the lake along paths of their own reflected light, spreading out in a wide arc like peacemakers being sent out. Each step leaves a small ring of light.

1:57 to 2:11 (strong, peak 2:07): the light goes to the whole world. The camera rises, the horizon gently curves, and beyond the mountains we glimpse distant lands under the night. One by one, small torch lights appear on far shores and hills, as the light is passed from people to people across the world. At the 2:07 peak, lines of light connect them across the curve of the earth like a net of gold, then settle.

### Verse 2, 2:12 to 3:04: the blue hour

Line 1, about 2:13 to 2:21, "dew of the dawn": fragment 2 (section 6). The sudden quiet is the most intimate moment of the song: a close, shallow-focus shot of one blade of grass at the shore, the lake soft behind it, silver mist on the water, the sky the cool teal of the blue hour.

Line 2, about 2:24, "the tree filled with water of life": the camera pulls back. A dewdrop falls into the lake and where it lands a seed of light glows on the shore. A tree of light grows from it in about eight seconds: trunk, branches, then leaves, each leaf a hanging drop of light. Living water rises from the lake into the roots and climbs the trunk as glowing sap, so we watch the water of life fill the tree from below. The tree stands left of center, its reflection long in the still water.

Line 3, about 2:34 to 2:41 (swell), "pure faith and beautiful love": the tree blossoms in white and gold, and soft luminous blossoms drift from it on the air over the whole lake. On the swell the drifting blossoms catch the first pale light of morning. No words here: the picture is the fragrance.

Line 4, about 2:46, "take after our Mother" and "bring life to this dying world": each young person takes a small branch of the tree's light. As they walk outward, their robes gently lengthen and fall in the same graceful lines as Heavenly Mother's robe on Her rise: children who take after their Mother. They carry the light to the far grey ridges, which until now have been dull and lifeless, and wherever they go the ridges warm into living green-gold and small lights bloom on the slopes like homes coming awake.

Line 5, about 2:57, "like a torch" (second time): no text. The branches burst into flame and become torches again, this time lit from the tree of life, warmer and steadier. The horizon blushes peach and the build into 3:05 is a slow brightening.

### Chorus 2, 3:05 to 3:59: the glorious dawn

About 3:06 to 3:10 (loudest moment), "O you stars of Zion": the morning star flares into its full cross of light, the last star before the sun, and every torch on the shore answers it. The sky floods with apricot and gold from the horizon up. Heavenly Mother's robed form glows warm against the dawn.

About 3:22 to 3:30, "Arise": fragment 3 (section 6). The sun rises with the word. This is the visual climax and the first sunrise of the concert. As the sun clears the mountains, far beyond them in the gold haze the faint, radiant outline of a heavenly city appears, towers and gates of soft light, distant and peaceful: the hope of the kingdom of heaven, the destination of the whole concert. It stays faint and far, never solid, from here to the end.

About 3:33 to 3:41, "power and strength": the echo of chorus 1, now in sunlight. Long golden sunbeams fall through the morning haze onto the young people, and their figures glow brighter and clearer, strengthened.

About 3:44 to 3:59, "Now let us arise": ENCOURAGEMENT (section 5). The full scene is described there. It ends on the final chord as the words become the star.

### Ending, 4:00 to 4:03, and the hold after the music

4:00: as the sound stops, the torches do not go out. Each flame softly lifts off its torch as a single spark, and all the sparks drift slowly upward toward the arch and melt into the morning sky, as if the young people's light has joined heaven's light.

Hold, from about 4:02 (about 3:08 live), as long as needed, seamless loop: a calm, radiant early morning. The sun just above the mountains, a long golden road on the water leading toward the far heavenly city, mist breathing on the lake, the young people standing quietly together along the shore in soft glow, Heavenly Mother's robed form on Her rise. The lake is perfectly still: peace has settled. The arch shows two lit stars and seven waiting seeds, all twinkling slowly; under each lit star its label shows very softly (COMPLIMENT, ENCOURAGEMENT) so the audience can name the two words they received. The title rests small in the top left.

## 5. The two words of Mother's love in this song

Both expressions come from the Visual Plan doc and fit the campaign's own meaning. The whole song is the voice of a parent praising and cheering on children. Compliment is heartfelt praise for effort and growth: verse 1 line 3 praises the young people's faithful work directly. Encouragement is sincere support for someone in a hard time, cheering them on: the final call to arise, with the promise that God has given power and strength, is exactly that. The other expressions are not the act of any line here and stay dark.

Each moment follows the same reverent pattern so the audience learns it in this first song: the everyday act happens between two people; the words appear in the upper third (small spaced capital label above, phrase in glowing warm-white serif with the expression's color glow, large enough to read from the back of the hall); the words hold for about 9 to 10 seconds with nothing else changing in the scene; peace settles (the water around the people goes perfectly still and the light warms); then the words gather into their star in the arch.

### Compliment (lavender #BE9CEB)

- Line: verse 1 line 3, cue words "faithful and beautiful work".
- Time: verse 1 line 3, about 0:38 to 0:48 (estimate; confirm by ear). Words on screen about 0:39 to 0:48, star forms about 0:48 to 0:50.
- On screen: label COMPLIMENT, phrase "You're amazing. You're doing great!"
- Everyday act, as the campaign teaches it: noticing someone's effort and progress and praising it from the heart. A young volunteer has worked longest on the shore; she straightens, tired, not expecting anything. Her friend looks at what she has done (the clean shore, the row of young trees), turns to her with an open hand, and praises her. She lifts her head; her shoulders rise. Tender and dignified, nothing exaggerated.
- Effect: the small sparks that have been rising from everyone's work gather softly in the upper third, lavender-tinted, and settle into the letters, as if every small faithful act became one letter of praise. As the words settle, a soft glow lights in the young woman's heart, and at the same instant a matching glow lights in her friend's heart: one kind word warming two hearts at once. Both glows breathe gently for the whole hold. Around them the ripples smooth away and the lake becomes a perfect mirror: peace settles. Then the words draw together into one lavender point, which rises along a gentle curve to its seed in the arch and opens into a star with a fine four-point glint and a soft lavender halo; one ring spreads on the water below.

### Encouragement (violet #B293E7)

- Line: the last "Now let us arise" of chorus 2.
- Time: about 3:44 to 3:59 (estimate inside the measured plateau 3:42 to 3:59). Words on screen about 3:48 to 3:58, collapsing on the final chord at about 3:58 to 4:00.
- On screen: label ENCOURAGEMENT, phrase "I'll pray (or root) for you. Everything will go well." (full wording, "(or root)" kept).
- Everyday act, as the campaign teaches it: standing beside someone in a hard moment and reminding them they have your support. During the sunbeams one young man has sunk to his knees, exhausted from the long night's work, his torch low. A friend sees him, comes, and kneels beside him on the shore so they are face to face in height, one hand on his shoulder and the other folded over his heart in prayer. Around them others turn and quietly raise their torches toward him, cheering him on. Then the friend stands and offers his hand, and the young man rises, his torch lifting with him.
- Effect: the phrase rises out of the sunrise itself: violet-edged gold sparks lift from the golden road on the water in a slow column and open into the two lines of the phrase. As the words settle, a warm violet light passes from the friend's hand into the kneeling young man, and we watch his glow grow from faint to full, the strengthening that comes when someone cheers you on. When he stands, every torch on the shore brightens at once, and the lake goes perfectly still in the morning light: peace settles. On the last brass chord the phrase folds into a violet point that rises to its seed in the arch and opens as a star with a brighter flash than the first, because it closes the opening song.
Arch positions (unchanged; for the continuity pass): this version still assumes the nine seeds are numbered left to right in campaign order, so Compliment is star 9 at the far right and Encouragement star 7, third from the right.

## 6. Lyric fragments on screen (three), each with its own effect

Version 1 had five fragments. To keep the two expressions the clearest words in the song, this version keeps only three, none within 15 seconds of an expression. Each forms, holds and leaves in its own way, and none repeats an effect from song 3. All appear in the upper third.

### Fragment 1: "like a torch" (about 1:02 to 1:09, verse 1 line 5)

Forms: as the flame passes hand to hand along the shore, a matching line of fire runs across the upper third from left to right, and each letter catches and stands as a small upright flame shaped like the letter, flickering gently, its reflection wavering in the lake. Holds: about 4 seconds, the flames breathing with the brass. Leaves: each letter lets go of its flame as a small rising ember; the embers drift up and vanish beneath the arch. Why: a torch is passed from one to another; the words are lit the same way the young people light each other, and the way Mother's words pass from person to person.

### Fragment 2: "dew of the dawn" (about 2:14 to 2:21, verse 2 line 1, the quietest moment; the promo uses its return in chorus 2)

Forms: in the close shot, fine silk threads of a dawn web hang across the upper third, already shaped as the letters but invisible in the dark. The first light of the blue hour runs along the threads like a spark along a fuse, and each letter appears as it is touched by light, silver and fine. Holds: about 3 seconds, the threads trembling slightly in the morning air. Leaves: a breath of wind lifts the web and the lit threads come loose as fine filaments that drift down to the lake, and where the last one touches the water the tree of life begins. (Changed in version 3: letters are no longer formed inside dewdrops, because songs 02, 03 and 05 use drops forming letters.) Why: dew is small, pure and new every morning, like the young, and like a small word of love it falls quietly and brings life.

### Fragment 3: "Arise" (about 3:22 to 3:30, chorus 2, on the measured lift)

Forms: first the word appears only as a golden reflection lying flat on the lake. Then, letter by letter, each letter stands up off the water, turning upright like a person rising from their knees, and climbs into the upper third while the sun breaks the horizon exactly behind it. Holds: about 4 seconds, the largest text in the song, sunlight glowing through the letters, the heavenly city appearing faintly beyond. Leaves: each letter stretches upward and becomes one of the first rays of the sunrise. Why: "arise" is the call of the whole song; the word does what it says, and the sun rises with it. It also prepares the Encouragement moment, where one friend helps another to rise. The reflection passes through the bottom third only as a faint shimmer for about two seconds.

## 7. The people of light and how they treat each other

- Who: young men and women of Zion as volunteers and peacemakers, about twenty along the shore at the widest, small in the frame so they read as a community. Only in the two expression moments do we come closer, to medium shots of two people.
- Made of light: formed from golden sparks with a soft glowing outline and a glassy inner glow. They begin as falling stars and unfold into people.
- Clothing: always clearly clothed. Young men in long-sleeved shirts and trousers or simple robes; young women in long modest dresses or robes; collars, sleeves and hems drawn in light, with soft folds that move. White-gold for most.
- Faces: heads are smooth glowing shapes with no features. Feeling is shown through posture and light: a head lifting, shoulders rising, a hand on a shoulder, a heart glowing.
- How they interact: always tender, dignified and joyful. They turn toward each other, kneel to the same height, offer an open hand, rise together. No one is ever left alone in the frame for long: every tired or lonely figure is soon joined. These are the everyday acts the campaign asks for, shown as if heaven were watching and delighted.
- The light of a heart: when a word of love is given, a soft glow lights in the chest of the giver and the receiver alike, and their outlines warm in color. This is the one visual sign, repeated in every expression of this song, that a word of Mother's love has passed between two people.
- Heavenly Mother: seen only from behind, a tall, fully robed figure of soft light on a gentle rise at the far right, never close, never a face. All warmth in the video flows first from Her direction. Her joy is shown by the sky warming above Her.
- God (Elohim): never shown as a figure; only as light descending from above the arch.

## 8. Moments to storyboard
