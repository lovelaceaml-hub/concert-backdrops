## 1. The idea in one paragraph
**"Where a mother's love reaches, peace settles."** This song asks us to save the world. The campaign shows how: not with great speeches, but one warm word at a time, passed from person to person, the way a mother's love passes to her children. So the video tells one story: Mother's love calls ordinary people out of the crowd, gives them strength in hard times, and sends them out to carry Her words to every shore on earth, and wherever those words arrive, the troubled water of the world grows still. It begins on the Lake of Galilee before dawn, where one fisherman is called. The same gold light that called Peter reaches the shore and lifts one grain of light out of countless grains, and that grain becomes a person who turns and greets the one beside them. In the night of the early church, the saints strengthen one another against the wind and then turn to us with the song's one expression, Encouragement, given room to breathe. At the end the sun rises over a world joined by threads of warm light in the colors of Mother's words, the gates of the heavenly kingdom glow above the clouds, and the lake lies as still as a mirror: peace. Because the live performance is a piano solo with no singing, the screen carries what the audience will not hear: a few short fragments and the one expression, so a listener can follow the story through the piano alone and leave knowing what Mother's words do.
## 2. What the campaign teaches this song
Studied November 13, 2026 from heavenlymotherslove.org (About, Checklist, Video, Peacemaker, Review, News, 2nd anniversary pages) and Anthony's campaign app (mothers-love.vercel.app and its Expressions page).
- **Peace is everyday and close at hand.** The campaign does not speak of peace as politics. Peace comes from small warm words between family, neighbors, coworkers and strangers: at a family meal, in an elevator, in an office, at a turnstile. So this song's "saving the world" is shown as people doing small acts of love for the person next to them, not as a crowd on a mountain.
- **A mother's love is the first love we receive.** Unconditional support, consideration, sacrifice and service, and it crosses every nation and culture. So Heavenly Mother is the source of every light in the video: the calling light that lifts the first grain comes from Her horizon, the gold ripples start where Her robe touches the water, and every thread leads back to where She stands.
- **Everyone is a peacemaker.** The campaign asks each person to carry the words into their own home, school and workplace, so that peacemakers bring hope and happiness to every corner of society. The song's "God called us" becomes this: each called grain of light turns at once to someone beside it.
- **One kind word warms two hearts.** The campaign describes kindness that spreads like a ripple. The engine's ripples on the water become the visual language of the song: each act of love sends out a warm ring, and where the rings pass, the water calms.
- **Encouragement means strength in a hard moment.** The campaign says knowing that people are cheering for you gives strength, and "(or root)" lets anyone cheer someone on, whether or not they pray. The Encouragement moment is built around exactly that: a tired person who receives strength, and lifts their head.
- **What real people describe.** The campaign's hundreds of shared stories tell of homes and workplaces made warmer, relationships repaired, and people who start with one word and find others start to say it too. The video's final movement shows this spreading.
- **The campaign's words of hope.** The campaign hopes warmth will reach every place in the world facing crisis and conflict. The song adds what the campaign pages do not speak of directly, the hope of the heavenly kingdom (the song's "day of glory of Heavenly Jerusalem"). The video joins them: the world made peaceful by Mother's words opens toward the glory of heaven.
## 3. Why this design fits
- **The lake is already Galilee.** Every song starts from the night lake with mountains. For this song that lake simply is the Lake of Galilee. No new world is needed, only boats, nets and a shore.
- **Sand is people.** The lyric compares the world's people to the sand on the seashore. The shore is made of countless grains of warm light, and "God called us" is one grain lifted on a beam of the calling light.
- **A piano is single notes.** A solo piano is one player and many single notes, like one person, one word, one act of love among many. Each note becomes one drop of light on the lake.
- **Still water is peace.** Throughout the video, troubled water (wind, chop, scattered reflections) means a troubled world, and still, mirror-like water means peace. Every act of Mother's love calms the water around it. By the end the whole lake is a mirror reflecting the sunrise and the gates of heaven.
- **Hardship becomes honor, night becomes dawn, dawn becomes glory.** The color journey follows the song: pre-dawn teal, grey-green sea mist in the night of the early church, then pale gold sunlight breaking through the mist and opening into the white-gold light of the heavenly kingdom. This is song 09's own look in the concert: a misty sea dawn at Galilee (teal, sea green, pearl and pale gold; morning mist and low cloud; glittering water; horizon mid and a wide sea).

### How the design adapts to the live piano solo
- **Seven movements, each with a resting hold.** Each movement ends on a calm, slowly moving scene that can loop without looking stuck (drifting mist, slow ripples, breathing stars). The operator can hold a movement until the pianist reaches the next section. A single continuous file is also made for the recording's timing.
- **The words follow the melody, not the singing.** Each fragment appears where its melody line is played. On a piano solo the fragments and the expression are how the song's words reach the audience at all.
- **If the pianist plays only one verse,** the short version keeps movements 1 to 4, then a compressed movement 5 (the saints and the Encouragement, about 35 seconds, the Encouragement still given its full 10 seconds) and the final movement 7.
- **Tempo** follows a rehearsal recording of the pianist's real arrangement once we have it (open question 1).
- **Ending:** the final scene holds as long as needed after the last note (section 10). It never goes black.
## 5. The video, movement by movement
All times are against the 4:34 recording (see section 4 for measured and estimated times); the live piano solo is planned at 4:36, so the 2 extra seconds go to the final hold. The bottom third of the screen stays calm throughout: water, mist and soft reflections only. Every interaction between people of light is tender, dignified and joyful: slow movements, open hands, heads that turn toward one another, and never anything hurried or dramatic.
### Movement 1. The lake before dawn (0:00 to 0:46, introduction)
**Scene:** the song opens on song 08's closing frame (a still golden lake, a first rose-gold glow low behind the mountains, all nine lights lit, no people). Over the first seconds a light wind rises, mist gathers and the glow cools to the hour fishermen come in, about 4:30 in the morning. A deep teal sky with a pearl band of mist above the mountains, and a wide sea with the horizon at mid height. Low mist breathes on the water. The warm star rests on the horizon. The water is restless: a light wind ruffles it, and the reflections of the stars are broken into a thousand trembling pieces. This is the world before peace. Three small fishing boats sit far out, each drawn in thin lines of light with one hanging lantern whose reflection shivers in the chop.
**Piano:** each note of the introduction drops a single bead of light onto the water, and a ring spreads from it. Higher notes fall farther away and are small; low notes land close and ring wide and slow. Where each ring passes, the chop smooths for a moment, as if the water is being comforted, then roughens again. The audience learns the song's language before a single word: love calms the water.
**Opening title (0:04 to 0:10):** one lantern boat glides slowly from left to right across the middle distance. A fisherman in its bow lifts the lantern high and, as it swings, its light leaves a glowing trail in the air above the boat that writes "Let's Save the World" in the warm-white serif, like writing with a lantern in the dark. The words drift up into the upper third, and the concert title "The Words of Mother's Love Healing Concert" fades in beneath, given equal warmth, so the audience reads the concert's message with the song's title. After about 5 seconds both drift to their small resting place top left.
**Camera:** low over the water, almost touching it, a very slow push toward the boats, mist drifting past the lens.
### Movement 2. The calling (0:47 to 1:11, fishermen and Simon Peter)
**Scene:** closer to the nearest boat. Two fishermen of light, in simple belted tunics and cloaks with the folds drawn in light, heads smooth and faceless, haul up a net of fine golden threads that drips beads of light. It has been a long night with nothing caught; their shoulders are tired. One steadies the other as the boat rocks: even before the call, a small kindness.
**The call (about 0:59):** the warm horizon star brightens, and a path of gold light runs across the water from the star to the nearest boat. Where the path lies, the water goes perfectly still, a gold road of calm across the restless lake. Christ is not shown as a figure; the call is the light. One fisherman lets the net slip back into the water, steps out of the boat onto the still gold path and stands facing the star. Before he goes, he turns back to his companion and rests a hand on his shoulder: he does not leave him behind without a word. The companion lifts his head toward the light too.
**Piano:** the net slipping away is timed to a held note or rest in the melody: silence in the music, stillness on screen.
**Camera:** a slow dolly around the boat, then a low shot from the water's surface looking up the gold path toward the star, the fisherman small and upright on it.
### Movement 3. As many as the sand (1:11 to 1:48, the many people, "God called us", the mission)
**Scene:** the camera rises slowly up and back, higher and higher, in one long unbroken crane move of about 25 seconds. The shore comes into view along the bottom edge, kept low and soft. Its sand is made of countless tiny grains of warm light, faintly flickering, like a city seen from the air at night. As the camera rises, the shore stretches beyond the lake and curves away to the horizon: the sand is the whole world's people. Each grain glows alone; none touches another.
**Fragment 1, "God called us" (about 1:24):** see section 8. A beam of the calling light lifts one grain.
**The first word (about 1:28):** the lifted grain is set down on the shore and unfolds into a small standing figure of light, robed and faceless. The very first thing it does is turn to the grain beside it, bend down and touch it gently, and that grain too unfolds into a person, and the two face each other. A small ring of warm light passes between them and the sand around them stops flickering and glows steady: the first greeting, the first word of Mother's love, the beginning of peace.
**The mission (1:30 to 1:48):** it spreads. Here and there along the shore, one at a time, each on its own piano note, a figure rises and turns to the next. Each pair is joined by a fine warm thread. By 1:46 small chains of figures stand along the shore, and behind each chain the sand has stopped flickering and lies in a soft steady glow.
**Color:** the deep teal lightens toward sea green; a thin line of pale gold appears above the mountains.
### Movement 4. Mother is life, the first dawn (1:48 to 2:13, chorus 1)
**Scene:** the camera lowers to a wide view across the lake. The horizon star swells and the first edge of the sun breaks the mountains. The whole sea turns from teal to pearl and pale gold, and the mist glows from within. The mountain skyline rises and falls with the music, as the engine already does.
**Heavenly Mother:** on a point of the shore, a single tall figure of light seen only from behind, Her robe and veil flowing in soft folds, stands facing the rising sun with Her arms opening slowly outward, as a mother opens her arms to children coming home. Where Her robe touches the water, gold ripples spread across the entire lake, and as they pass, the chop dies away and the water becomes still enough to hold the whole sunrise in its reflection. All the small figures along the shore turn toward Her. The warmth reaches them; their glow deepens from pale to rich gold, as if warmed by a hearth. This is the warming, strengthening love of a mother, shown as light and stillness.
**Fragment 2, "hope for all humankind" (about 1:55):** see section 8.
**Piano:** each strong chord sends a wide gold ring outward from where She stands.
**Camera:** a slow push from behind Her shoulder toward the sun, so the audience stands where She stands and sees what She sees: the whole shore of Her children.
### Movement 5. The night of the early church (2:13 to 3:34, interlude and verse 2)
**Transition (2:13 to 2:30):** as the first chorus ends and the music quiets, time turns back. The sunrise softly sinks, the stars return, and the lake goes back to night, but a different night: thick grey-green sea mist, with the lamps the only warm color. Wind rises; the water becomes rough again. This is the age of the early church, long ago.
**The saints (2:30 to 2:42):** on the far shore, a procession of figures made of pale gold light walks slowly along the water's edge, each carrying a small clay oil lamp. Long robes and hooded cloaks drawn in soft gold lines, half hidden in the mist. The wind is visible as streams of sparks blowing against them; their lamps flicker but never go out. They do not walk alone: when one stumbles in the wind, the one behind steadies them with a hand on the back; when a lamp gutters, a neighbor shelters it with a cupped hand until it burns again; an older figure leans on a younger one's arm. Nothing shows violence or death. Their strength is in how they hold each other up, with calm, dignity and quiet joy.
**Fragment 3, "hardships as honor" (about 2:39):** see section 8.
**Their souls call out to us (2:42 to 2:55):** the procession stops. All the figures turn toward the viewer, toward us, and lift their lamps. Their faces stay smooth light. The lamps brighten together, and the wind drops.
**The Encouragement (2:55.5 to 3:05.5, ten full seconds, nothing else moving):** see section 7. This is the heart of the song.
**After the words (3:06 to 3:20):** for a moment there are no words at all. The great light of glory opens behind the mountains: a vast, soft column of white-gold light, so tall it reaches past the top of the screen. Beside it, the last small storm clouds of the night look tiny, and as the light spreads they melt into it. The line about present sufferings that cannot compare to the glory ahead is shown without words, as this change of scale.
**With joy, the day of glory (3:20 to 3:34):** the procession walks on toward that light with lighter steps. As each figure passes beyond the right edge of the screen, it rises as a stream of sparks into the sky: they have gone ahead into glory. High in the clouds above the mountains, for the first time, the faint outline of a city of light appears, its gates glowing softly: Heavenly Jerusalem, the day of glory the song looks toward. The mist thins to pearl, then the first teal and pale gold of a new dawn.
**Camera:** a slow side-track walking beside the procession on the near shore, then a gentle tilt upward to the city in the clouds.
### Movement 6. The world wakes (3:34 to 3:48, chorus 2)
**Scene:** full sunrise, brighter and wider than in movement 4. The shore of light is full now: thousands of small robed figures along every shore, the near one and the far one, and beyond the mountains along a curved horizon that suggests the round earth.
**Mother's words travel:** the threads between people now carry color. Each thread pulses with the warm color of one of the Words of Mother's Love, as the warmth passes from one person to the next. No words are written; the colors and the small gestures tell it. Close to camera, a few everyday moments play out in passing, each brief and tender: one figure bends to help an older figure carry a heavy bundle; two figures who stood apart turn, bow their heads and embrace; one figure steps aside with an open hand to let another go first; one figure sits and listens to another, leaning in; a figure puts a hand on a tired shoulder; a small figure runs to a parent and is lifted up. Each act sends a warm ring across the water, and the water around it goes still. These are the everyday scenes the campaign describes, at home, at work, on the street, in the language of light. (These colored threads are a background picture of Mother's words spreading; no new expression words appear and no new star is lit here. The continuity pass can adjust the colors.)
**Heavenly Mother:** again only from behind, at the center of the near shore, Her robe lit by the full sun. The threads all lead back to where She stands. High above, the city of light in the clouds is brighter now, and the arch of lights glows above it.
**Camera:** a slow rising push forward over the water toward the sun, the most open and sweeping move of the song, passing low over the small everyday moments as if we are walking among them.
### Movement 7. Let's save the world, the net of light (3:48 to 4:32, final line and outro)
**The pause (3:48):** at the measured sharp dip, everything holds still for one breath. The threads stop pulsing. The whole lake is calm.
**Fragment 4, "Let's save the world" (3:50 to 4:06, the song's peak):** see section 8. The net of light is cast across the sky.
**Peace settles (4:06 to 4:32):** the morning settles. The sun is up, the gates of the city above the clouds glow softly, and the lake is now a perfect mirror: no wind, no chop, every reflection whole for the first time in the song. The mirror holds the sunrise, the arch, the city of light and the shore of joined people all at once. The boats from the opening are back on the water, each with a figure of light standing at its bow, looking outward, ready to go, fishers of people carrying Mother's words. The piano's last notes drop single beads of light that ring out across the mirror and fade without disturbing it. The audience sees what the campaign promises: where a mother's love reaches, peace settles.

## 7. The expression: the heart of the song
**Encouragement (violet #B293E7): "I'll pray (or root) for you. Everything will go well."**
**Line:** "Pain and sufferings are our destiny, but Heaven's glory waits for us." Measured 2:55.5 to 3:08, right after the saints' souls call out to us.
**Why this line:** the campaign gives Encouragement as sincere support for someone in a hard moment, because knowing someone is cheering you on gives strength. In this verse, the saints who went before us call out to us in our hardship and tell us glory waits. That is exactly the act of saying "Everything will go well" to someone who is struggling. Other lines were considered and not used: "let us repay the grace of God" is a promise to repay grace, not words of thanks, so even under the concert rule that Gratitude includes thanks to Father and Mother it is not shown; "God called us" is a calling, not one of the nine words; the chorus is a proclamation.
**The moment, in four beats (about 10 seconds, nothing else moving on screen, no fragment before or after for several seconds):**
- **1. The light crosses time (2:55.5).** The saints hold their lamps toward us. From each lamp a small violet flame drifts forward across the dark water, from the ancient far shore toward the near shore: from their time to ours.
- **2. A tired heart today (2:56.5 to 2:59.5).** On the near shore, close to camera but kept above the bottom third, sits one present-day figure of light in modern everyday clothes, a coat and scarf, knees drawn up, head bowed, glowing dimly: someone at the end of a hard day, worn out and alone. Beside them a second figure, a friend, sits down quietly, close but not crowding, and rests a hand on their shoulder.
- **3. The words (2:59.5 to 3:04.5).** The violet flames gather above the two of them in the upper third and settle into the label ENCOURAGEMENT in small spaced capitals and beneath it the full phrase in the glowing warm-white serif with a soft violet halo: "I'll pray (or root) for you. Everything will go well." As the words complete, the tired figure slowly lifts their head and turns toward the friend. Their dim glow strengthens from the center outward, from pale to warm gold, like a fire catching. Around the two of them the rough water goes still in a widening circle that reflects the words.
- **4. Strength rises (3:04.5 to 3:05.5 and on).** The tired figure stands, and the friend stands with them. The words gather into a single violet point of light that rises gently into the arch, and the two figures watch it go, side by side.
**Why it is built this way:** the campaign's whole point is that these words are said by ordinary people to each other in everyday life. So the saints' encouragement does not stay in the past: it passes to a friend sitting beside someone on an ordinary hard day, and the audience sees what the words do. Strength returns, the person stands, and the water grows still. Nothing competes with it: no fragment within several seconds, the camera still, the saints holding their lamps.
**Which star:** star 7, Encouragement. All nine lights are already lit when this song begins (lit across songs 01 to 08). Here star 7 is lit again: the violet light rises into it, and it gives a brief flare and gains one new thin ring (the concert rule for a light lit again). The arch ends the song complete, with star 7 freshly ringed.
## 8. The lyric fragments on screen
Four short fragments, reduced from six so the Encouragement has the song to itself. Each has its own effect. All appear in the upper third in the concert serif, warm white unless noted, and leave the bottom third clear. (Version 1's "like the sand" and "cannot even compare" are now shown only in pictures, without words: the shore of grains, and the change of scale beside the light of glory.)
### Fragment 1. "God called us" (about 1:24)
**Effect: lifted on the calling light.** The shore is still and full. The gold path that called Peter runs on across the lake to the shore, and on a single clear piano note one thin beam of it touches one grain. The grain lifts gently on the beam, the way a mother lifts a sleeping child, and as it rises a fine thread of light trails from it and writes the words in one continuous line of handwriting in the sky, like a name being written. When the last letter is done, the grain hangs for a moment at the end of the line, glowing warmer than any grain on the shore, then floats back down.
**Why:** out of countless grains, one is chosen, and the campaign teaches that every person's first love is a mother's. Being called is shown as being lifted and held by the light that comes from Her.
### Fragment 2. "hope for all humankind" (about 1:55, chorus 1)
**Effect: written by the people.** As Heavenly Mother opens Her arms and the sun rises, the camera lifts high above the shore. The newly called figures, each holding a small light, step to their places along the water's edge, and seen from above their lights spell the words, like a hillside of candles. A breath later the camera tilts up and the same words appear large in the sky above them, warm gold and pearl, as if the people's lights had written them on the morning. They hold, then fade slowly into the brightening sky.
**Why:** the hope for all humankind is carried by people. The words are made by the children Mother called, standing together, so the hope the chorus sings is something the audience sees people becoming.
### Fragment 3. "hardships as honor" (about 2:39, verse 2)
**Effect: the forge.** The words appear above the procession as if cast in dark iron, dull and heavy, edged with faint red. With each of three slow piano chords they heat from within: deep red, then orange, then bright gold-white, with a few sparks spitting off each time. At full heat the iron is gone; the words are pure gold light. They cool to a warm glow and fade.
**Why:** the saints counted hardship as honor. The same heat that is hardship turns iron into something glowing and precious.
### Fragment 4. "Let's save the world" (3:50 to 4:06, the song's peak)
**Effect: the net cast across the sky.** A figure of light in the bow of the near boat, like the fisherman of the opening, draws back and casts a great net, as fishermen do, but the net flies upward and opens across the whole sky, a shimmering mesh of fine threads catching the morning light at every knot. The knots glow in the warm colors of the Words of Mother's Love, and the brightest of them spell "Let's save the world" across the middle of the sky. On the song's peak (about 4:02) the whole net flashes once, every knot sends a thread down to the people on the shores below, and the net softens and drifts down over the lake and the land like dew. The words fade with it, and the lake beneath becomes a mirror.
**Why:** the song begins with fishermen and nets. The net that once caught fish now carries Mother's words to every person, and the world it touches is at peace. It ties the last line to the first scene, and it is the biggest image of the song at its loudest moment.
## 9. People made of light
- **Style:** the concert style from DESIGN-SYSTEM.md: formed from golden sparks with a soft outline and a glassy inner glow. Always clearly clothed, the folds of robes, tunics and coats drawn in light. Heads are smooth glowing shapes with no facial features. Feeling is shown by posture: a bowed head, a lifted head, a hand on a shoulder, two figures turning toward each other.
- **Interactions:** tender, dignified and joyful. Slow and unhurried, open hands, gentle touches on shoulder or arm, embraces between people who were apart, a child lifted by a parent. Every act of kindness sends a warm ring across the water and calms it.
- **Fishermen:** belted knee-length tunics and cloaks, working posture. Peter is no larger than the others: he is ordinary.
- **Christ:** not shown as a figure. The calling is the gold path of light.
- **The called (sand figures):** small at distance, many sizes and ages, all nations suggested by a variety of simple clothing, without detail.
- **The early church saints:** long robes and hooded cloaks in soft gold light, each with a small clay oil lamp. They hold one another up. Nothing shows violence, wounds or death.
