# **02 The Rose: Video Design**

**Working title of the design: "The Seed Under the Snow"**  
"The Words of Mother's Love Healing Concert" (poster title), Sunday December 6, 2026, 4:30 PM, 6720 W Sahara Ave, Las Vegas. Song 2 of 11\. Performed live by a female trio, female choir and piano. Live length 4:00. Video on the screen behind the performers, 1920x1080, 30 fps.  
Status: design proposal, awaiting Anthony's approval. Written November 13, 2026 by a Claude Code design session; revised the same day after a full study of the Mother's Love campaign (version 2), then the continuity pass (version 3): 4:00 live length, the concert star history, no Greeting in this song, the poster title, live playback by an operator, and the agreed hand-offs (DESIGN-SYSTEM.md 12a). A 1-minute visual-only promo exists (section 17); the full video has not been rendered. There is no recording of this song in the project folder yet, so every time below is an estimate and must be re-timed to the live trio arrangement.  
**The message this video carries:** the Words of Mother's Love bring peace. In song 2 the audience sees one of those words, Encouragement, "I'll pray (or root) for you. Everything will go well.", spoken to someone in their hardest night, and sees with their own eyes that the spoken word is what wakes the seed and brings the spring.

# **1\. The idea in one paragraph**

The song says that love is not a river that drowns or a blade that cuts, but a seed: hidden, small, waiting, and certain to bloom. The campaign says the same thing about a mother: from the day we are born she plants seeds of hope in our hearts. In this video the warm star that always rests on the horizon of our night lake slowly sinks down to the shore and becomes a glowing seed: Mother's love planted in us. Winter arrives. Diamond snow falls, the lake freezes into a mirror of stars, and a lonely traveler crosses the ice through the long night until they can walk no more. Three women of light, one for each voice of the trio, come down from the sky, kneel beside the traveler, relight their lantern and wrap them in warmth, and the Words of Mother's Love appear: "I'll pray (or root) for you. Everything will go well." Then something wonderful happens: the violet light of those words does not only rise into the sky; part of it sinks down through the snow, all the way to the seed, and the seed wakes. The words are what bring the spring. The sun rises, two vast hands of golden light open in the dawn clouds and pour their warmth over the land, the ice melts into a shining lake, and a rose made of light opens on the exact spot where the star went down. The star returns to the sky, the sky beyond the mountains glows like the gates of a heavenly home, and the rose stays on the shore for the rest of the concert. Practicing the Words of Mother's Love turns the longest winter into spring.

# **2\. What the campaign teaches this song**

Studied: heavenlymotherslove.org (About, Daily Check, Video, Peacemaker, Review, News, 2nd anniversary pages) and Anthony's campaign app mothers-love.vercel.app with its Expressions page. What shaped this revision:

* **A mother plants seeds of hope.** The campaign describes a mother planting seeds of hope in her children's hearts from birth, and speaks of happiness that blooms in everyday life. The Rose's own image of the seed under the snow becomes, in this video, exactly that: Mother's love planted in us, alive even in winter, blooming when the words are practiced.  
* **Encouragement in everyday life.** The campaign defines it as sincere support for someone going through a hard time, so they know people are cheering for them. The everyday picture is simple: noticing someone who is struggling, coming close, and telling them you are with them. The interaction in verse 3 is built on that: coming down to the traveler's level, relighting their lantern, covering their shoulders, praying for them. The hold after the music then shows small everyday scenes of the same word (see section 15).  
* **Peace settles where the love reaches.** The campaign's core line, "Where a mother's love reaches, peace settles", and its language of waves of change spreading outward shaped the key effect: the violet light of the words travels outward and downward until it reaches the seed, and peace (spring) settles over the whole lake from that point.  
* **Warmth that melts the heart.** The campaign's most frequent language is warmth: warm words, a phrase that melts the heart, hearts warmed at once. In this song warmth is literal: the words melt the ice.  
* **Small words, great change.** The campaign stresses that these are ordinary words in ordinary places (elevators, kitchens, offices, queues) and that one kind word can warm two hearts at once. So the encouragers in this song are not angels or heroes; they are ordinary women in simple clothes, and the traveler is anyone. Both the giver and the receiver glow brighter after the words are said.  
* **The Greeting is "the first phrase to peace". By the concert plan (DESIGN-SYSTEM.md 12a) Greeting is first lit in song 07, where a lyric line is that act; song 02 shows no Greeting.**

# **4\. The song's meaning, and how it becomes Mother's love**

* **Verse 1**. What it says (in our words): People describe love in painful ways: something that sweeps you away, something that wounds, a need that never ends. The singer answers with a gentler picture: love is something that grows and blooms, from a seed each of us carries. What it means for this concert: The world's ideas of love are cold and frightening. A mother's love is different: it is a seed placed gently in our hands.  
* **Verse 2**. What it says (in our words): A run of images about fear: a heart that protects itself from hurt misses joy, a dreamer who will not risk misses the dream, someone closed to receiving cannot give, and a soul that fears loss never really lives. What it means for this concert: Fear keeps the heart closed. Mother's love is what gives the courage to open it: to receive, to give, to live.  
* **Verse 3**. What it says (in our words): The singers speak to someone exhausted by a lonely night and a long road, who has begun to believe love is only for other people. They tell them to remember that under the deep winter snow a seed is waiting, and the warmth of spring will make it bloom into a rose. What it means for this concert: The singer speaks straight to someone who is worn out and alone, and promises them it will be alright. This is the exact act of Encouragement. The sun's love that wakes the seed is Heavenly Mother's love.

# **6\. The world and how it transforms**

The song begins in the concert's home world: the still night lake, the long mountain silhouette and its reflection, the starry sky, warm floating particles, and the warm star resting on the horizon. Across four minutes this one place passes through a whole year in reverse order of hope: **night, then winter, then the deepest night of winter, then spring.** Nothing cuts away. Every change happens on screen, slowly, as if the audience is watching one place through one long night.

1. **Morning fading into the night lake (the home scene). Song 1's morning colors pass through dusk over the piano introduction and settle into the calm, familiar night. The arch from song 1 glows faintly at the top of the sky, stars 9 and 7 lit.**  
2. **First snow.** One snowflake, then a few, then a quiet steady fall. Each flake is a tiny point of cool light that catches a glint of gold as it passes the horizon star.  
3. **The star goes down.** At the end of verse 1 the horizon star leaves its place for the first time in the concert. It drifts down like a falling seed, lands on the near shore (left of center, in the middle third of the screen) and settles into the earth, leaving only a soft warm glow under the ground.  
4. **The freeze.** During verse 2 and the interlude, frost grows across the lake from the shoreline inward in feathery crystal patterns. The reflection of the mountains becomes blurred and silver, then disappears under ice. Snow settles on the ice. The world becomes silent and blue.  
5. **The longest night, and the mirror of stars.** At the start of verse 3 the sky is at its darkest and the arch above is dim. But even this winter is beautiful: the snow stops for a moment and the frozen lake becomes a perfect mirror of the night sky, so the traveler walks across a field of stars, a second heaven under their feet. Fine diamond dust glitters in the air. The only warm things on screen are the faint glow under the snow where the seed lies and the traveler's tiny lantern. The message of the image: even in the loneliest night, heaven is closer than we think.  
6. **The descent.** The camera sinks down through the snow, through the frozen earth, and finds the seed: a small golden shape, pulsing like a heartbeat, with fine roots of light already reaching down. It was never dead. It was waiting. A thread of violet light from the words spoken above arrives and wraps around it, and the heartbeat quickens.  
7. **Spring.** The camera rises again. A thin line of rose-gold light appears behind the mountains, then long rays of light fan out across the whole sky. High in the dawn clouds, two vast open hands of golden light (Heavenly Mother's hands, the only form in which she appears) slowly open, palms toward the earth, as if warming the land the way a mother warms a child's cold hands between her own. Warm light pours down from them. Wherever it touches, the ice glows from within, cracks softly into plates and melts into shining water; the reflection of the mountains returns in gold. Snow on the shore turns into dew and thousands of tiny pale blossoms of light open across the hillsides. From the place where the seed lay, a stem of light rises and a rose opens.  
8. **The star returns, and heaven glows.** As the rose opens fully, a small warm light lifts out of its center and floats back to its old place on the horizon. Behind the mountains, where the sun is rising, the clouds part into a radiant golden distance, layered and endless, like the first glimpse of a heavenly home: no buildings or gates are drawn, only light so deep and peaceful that it feels like a promise. The home world is whole again, now in glorious early spring dawn, and the rose stays on the shore.

# **8\. Camera feel**

* **Intimate and still. This is the most intimate song of the night: three voices and a piano. The camera behaves like someone holding their breath. A close, quiet frame (closer than any other song's home view, horizon at mid height) with very slow push-ins (about 5 percent closer per verse), never a fast move.**  
* **One big move.** The only dramatic move of the song is the descent beneath the snow in verse 3 and the rise back up into spring. It should feel like sinking into a quiet thought and then lifting into sunlight. The rise continues past the surface into a slow, majestic crane upward that reveals the whole thawing lake, the rose on the shore, the golden hands in the clouds and the arch of lights in one glorious wide frame: the most magnificent shot of the song, held long enough to take in.  
* **Close and tender for people.** During the Encouragement the camera glides in low over the ice, at the height of the kneeling traveler, so the audience is down beside them, then holds completely still for the whole expression moment.  
* **Gentle drift with the piano.** On long piano phrases the frame may drift slightly sideways, as if floating on the water.  
* **Depth.** Snow in three layers (near, middle, far) moves at different speeds so the screen feels deep even when the camera is still. Near flakes are larger and softly out of focus.  
* **Bottom third.** The frozen lake and later the water fill the bottom third. Snow is light there, nothing bright or busy, so the trio and choir stand in front of a calm surface. All figures, words and the rose sit in the middle and upper thirds.

# **9\. How the trio, choir and piano drive the picture**

* **Piano \= snow and frost.** Each piano note (the engine's note onsets) makes one snowflake near the camera catch a small glint of light. Before the lake freezes, piano notes also make soft rings on the water, as in song 3\. After the freeze, each note makes a small frost crystal bloom on the ice instead of a ring. The audience hears the piano and sees it sparkle.  
* **The trio \= three ribbons of light.** Three thin aurora ribbons live high in the sky: warm white, pale gold and soft rose. They are the three singers. When the trio sings in unison the ribbons braid into one; when they open into harmony the ribbons separate and weave around each other. Their brightness follows loudness. In verse 3 they come down to earth and become three figures (see section 10).  
* **The female choir \= the field of lights.** When the choir joins, the snow lying on the ice begins to glow, flake by flake, until the whole frozen lake shines like a field of tiny candles. The choir is the warmth of many people around the lonely one.  
* **Loudness \= the dawn.** The engine's sky glow follows the recording. In this song it is held very low until the last line, so that the sunrise arrives exactly with the musical climax.  
* **Silence \= stillness.** If the arrangement has a pause or a held breath before the last line, every particle on screen slows almost to a stop, and starts again with the next note.  
* Practical note: the engine reads one mixed recording, so it cannot hear the three voices separately. Unison and harmony moments for the ribbons will be set by hand from the rehearsal recording.

# **10\. Section by section**

## **Hand-over and title (about 0:00 to 0:20)**

We arrive from song 1's bright, brass-filled ending through a 6 to 8 second crossfade in which only the arch stays fixed. We begin in song 1's morning colors, and with the first piano notes the sun sinks and the sky darkens through dusk into night. Stillness. The night lake, the mountains, the horizon star. With the first piano notes, a single snowflake falls from the top of the screen, slowly turning, catching the starlight. More follow. The opening title forms (described in section 14).

## **Verse 1 (about 0:20 to 1:15): the wrong pictures of love, and the seed**

As the trio describes the painful ideas people have of love, the lake's reflection quietly shows three cold pictures, one per idea, each lasting only a few seconds and each dissolving before the next. They appear only in the water, never in the sky, as if they are just reflections of the world's thinking, not truth.

* The calm reflection is pulled into a fast dark current, as if the lake has become a river rushing away.  
* A single thin silver line cuts across the reflection of the mountains and the reflection splits apart, then slowly closes again.  
* A hollow of darkness opens in the water where the reflection of the star should be, as if something empty is swallowing the light.

These are gentle, not frightening: shapes and light only, no violence, nothing graphic. Then the singer's gentler answer comes: love grows and blooms, from a seed we carry. At that line, for the first time in the concert, the horizon star moves. It lifts from the horizon, glides down across the lake like a falling seed with a soft golden tail, and comes to rest on the near shore, left of center. It sinks into the ground and leaves a faint warm glow under the earth. The reflections in the water return to calm. The audience should feel a small surprise: the star we have seen all night has gone somewhere, and they will want to know where.

## **Verse 2 (about 1:15 to 2:05): the heart afraid to break**

Snow falls steadily now. On the shore stands a single figure made of light: a young woman in a long winter coat and scarf, drawn in soft gold sparks, faceless, about one fifth of the screen height. She holds a small warm light against her chest with both hands, the way someone protects a candle from the wind. That light is her heart. She is afraid to let it go.  
Frost begins to spread across the lake from the shore.  
At the line about the heart that never dances, a single point of gold light glides onto the new ice and skates across it in long, graceful curving loops, like a figure skater, leaving glowing trails cut into the frost; the snowflakes around it lift and turn in a slow waltz circle. No words appear: the light simply dances where she cannot. The figure looks up at it, and for a moment she loosens her hands. Her light flickers, tempted.  
Through the next lines (more images of fear holding the heart back) she stays still and the snow thickens. Only at the very end of the verse, on the idea of learning to live, does she slowly lift her cupped hands upward, toward the sky, and open them a little. Her light rises a few inches and hovers above her palms. She has not let go yet, but she has begun. The verse ends with her standing in the snow holding her open hands up, her small light floating just above them.  
No expression appears in this verse. The lines are about fear and courage, which is the heart of the song but not any one of the nine acts.

## **Interlude (about 2:05 to 2:25): the lake freezes**

If the arrangement has a piano interlude, this is where the world goes fully into winter. The frost reaches the far shore. The reflection of the mountains fades under the ice. The figure from verse 2 dissolves gently into sparks that drift into the snow. The arch at the top of the sky dims to its faintest. The sky becomes the darkest it has been in the concert. If there is no interlude, this freeze happens over the last lines of verse 2 instead.

## **Verse 3, first half (about 2:25 to 2:55): the long lonely night**

Far out on the frozen lake, a tiny figure appears: a traveler of light in a hooded cloak, walking slowly from the right side toward the shore where the seed lies. Behind them, a long line of footprints glows faintly and fades. The road is very long; the traveler is very small. Snow blows across the frame. The traveler's own light, a small lantern held low, flickers in the wind.  
The fragment **"too lonely"** appears here (section 12). As it fades, the traveler stops and sinks to one knee in the snow, head bowed. The whole screen is at its coldest and quietest.  
Then the female choir enters (if the arrangement has it here). One by one the snowflakes lying on the ice around the traveler begin to glow softly, as if many unseen people are lighting candles in the dark. The traveler does not see it yet, but the audience does: they were never alone.

## **Verse 3, second half (about 2:55 to 3:30): Encouragement, and the seed**

Now the trio turns to the lonely one and tells them to remember. At this line the three aurora ribbons in the sky (the three voices) pour down from the heavens like falling silk and land softly around the kneeling traveler. Each ribbon gathers into a figure of light: three ordinary women in simple long coats and scarves, faceless, one warm white, one pale gold, one soft rose. Their arrival is gentle, not grand: they come the way a friend comes when she hears you are struggling.  
What they do is tender and everyday, the way the campaign describes Encouragement:

* The first kneels in the snow so she is at the traveler's level, and with both hands lifts the traveler's nearly dark lantern. She holds her own small light to it, and the traveler's flame catches again and grows.  
* The second takes off her scarf of light and wraps it around the traveler's shoulders, smoothing it the way a mother tucks in a child.  
* The third stands just behind them, head bowed, hands folded in prayer: the "pray" of the words made visible.

Then, as the trio sings the reassurance, everything else on screen goes still for the expression moment (about 9 seconds): the snow slows almost to a hover, the ribbons are gone from the sky, no fragment or other effect competes. The expression appears: **ENCOURAGEMENT** as the small spaced label, with "I'll pray (or root) for you. Everything will go well." in glowing warm-white serif with a violet glow, in the upper third (effect in section 11). As the words hold, the traveler slowly raises their head, then reaches up and puts one hand over the kneeling woman's hand on the lantern. Both of their lights brighten at once: one kind word warms two hearts.  
The traveler rises with their help. The four figures stand together, the traveler's arm linked with one of the women, and turn toward the shore, toward the warm glow under the snow. A soft violet wave spreads outward from where they stand, across the whole frozen lake, and the field of choir lights brightens as it passes: where the words reach, peace settles.  
Then the trio sings of what lies far beneath the snow, and the camera follows the violet light down. It tilts down and sinks slowly through the surface of the snow, through blue-white layers of packed snow lit from above, into the dark earth, and finds the seed: a small golden shape, the star from verse 1, pulsing softly in time with the piano, with fine roots of light reaching downward and one tiny shoot curled upward, waiting. A thread of violet light from the words arrives from above and wraps once around the seed. The fragment **"the seed"** appears here (section 12). The pulse of the seed gets faster and warmer, like a heart that has heard its name. The words spoken above have reached it.

## **Final line and tag (about 3:30 to 4:00): the sun's love, and the rose**

On the sun's love, the camera rises back up through the earth and snow into the open air, and the world has changed: a thin rose-gold line is spreading behind the mountains, and long rays of light fan up across the whole sky. As the music builds to its last line, two vast hands of golden light open in the dawn clouds, palms toward the earth, and their warmth pours down like a mother warming a cold child. The sun's first rays sweep across the lake. The ice glows from within, breaks softly into plates that sink and melt into clear water; the mountains' reflection returns, now in pink and gold. Snow on the shore becomes beads of dew, and small blossoms of light open across the hillsides one after another, spreading out from the shore like a wave. The four figures of light stand together at the shore's edge, faces lifted to the light, the traveler standing tall now with an arm around one of the women. The camera begins its slow, majestic crane upward.  
From the spot where the seed lay, a stem of light rises, unfolds two leaves, and a bud forms. On the final words the rose opens, petal by petal, deep crimson at its heart and warm rose-gold at its edges, with dew drops shining on it. The fragment **"becomes the rose"** appears in the dew (section 12). The traveler kneels beside the rose, cups their hands around it without touching it, and turns to the three women: the one who was helped now shares the joy. All four glow brighter.  
As the last piano notes play alone, a small warm light lifts out of the center of the open rose and floats back across the lake to its old place on the horizon. The star is home. The golden hands in the clouds slowly close, as if gathering everyone in, and soften into the sunrise. Behind the mountains the clouds part into a radiant, endless golden distance, a glimpse of a heavenly home made only of light. The three women and the traveler walk together along the shore toward that light, growing smaller, then dissolve into sparks that rise and fade among the last stars of the dawn sky. The rose remains.

The Visual Plan suggested "How are you?" at the long lonely night. In the song, though, no one says hello: the singers are encouraging someone who is worn out, which is the act of Encouragement, already shown at that moment. Following the rule that an expression appears only where a line is exactly that act, song 02 shows no Greeting. The concert plan (DESIGN-SYSTEM.md 12a, provisional while Anthony is away) lights Greeting first in song 07\. The sample picture 02-b shows a greeting scene; it is a still sample only and does not change this.

## **How the Encouragement words appear**

The moment lasts about 9 seconds and nothing else moves or speaks on screen. The three women each breathe out a thread of light: warm white, pale gold, soft rose. The three threads rise and braid together above the traveler, and where they braid, violet sparks bloom like frost flowers opening. The sparks gather into the label ENCOURAGEMENT and then into the full phrase, letter by letter, as if three voices were writing it together. The phrase holds, softly breathing with the music, with a gentle violet warmth glowing on the snow beneath it; flakes that drift through the words turn violet and then gold as they melt. Then the words divide into two lights. The larger part spirals upward in a slow column of violet light into star 7, already lit since song 01; when it arrives star 7 gives a brief flare and gains one new thin ring (the concert's rule for a light lit again), and star 9 beside it glimmers once in welcome. The smaller part falls like a single warm drop onto the snow, and a violet wave spreads outward from it across the lake and down through the snow toward the seed. This second light is the heart of the design: the word does not only go up to heaven; it also reaches down into the person's winter and wakes what Mother planted there.  
**Star 7 was lit by song 01 at "Now let us arise". Encouragement returns in songs 02, 03, 05 and later, and each return adds one thin ring, so by the finale star 7 carries the most rings in the arch: the word the concert says most often to people in hard times.**

# **12\. On-screen fragments**

Three short fragments only, each two or three words, each with its own effect, so the Words of Mother's Love stay the clearest words on screen. None appears within 10 seconds of the expression. Font: the concert's glowing warm-white serif, unless noted. All sit in the middle or upper third.

## **Fragment 1: "too lonely" (verse 3, about 2:35)**

**Effect: one lantern's breath in the cold.** The traveler holds up their small lantern. Its light breathes out a cloud of warm mist into the freezing air, the way breath shows on a winter night, and the words appear inside the mist, soft and slightly blurred. The wind takes the mist sideways and the letters stretch and thin as they drift away from the traveler, growing fainter and farther apart, until they are scattered into separate letters and then nothing. The traveler's lantern dims a little after them.  
**Why:** loneliness feels like your own warmth being blown away into the dark with no one to receive it. The words leave the traveler and are lost, which makes the audience long for someone to come. Then the choir's field of lights answers, and the three women come: the loneliness is the question, and the Words of Mother's Love are the answer.

## **Fragment 2: "the seed" (verse 3, under the snow, about 3:15)**

**Effect: written in roots.** Under the earth, the fine golden roots of the seed grow outward and slowly spell the two words around the seed, like roots finding their way through soil, branching into the shape of each letter. The letters glow brighter on every heartbeat pulse of the seed. They never leave the ground: when the camera rises, the words stay buried below, and the audience understands that they are the life hidden under the winter.  
**Why:** what Mother plants in us is not visible on the surface. It grows in the dark, quietly, before anything blooms. This is the only fragment the audience sees underground, and it glows brightest at the instant the violet light of the Encouragement reaches it: the seed wakes because the words were spoken.

## **Fragment 3: "becomes the rose" (final line, about 3:50)**

**Effect: dew drops catching the sunrise.** As the rose opens, tiny dew drops on its petals and on the leaves catch the first sunlight one by one, in order, like lights switching on. The lit drops float up off the flower, gather above it in the upper third, and arrange themselves into the words, each letter made of shining drops with the sunrise glinting through them in rose and gold. The words hold through the last note. Then the drops fall gently back onto the petals, and the rose shines brighter for having received them.  
**Why:** the snow of winter becomes the dew of spring, and the cold that hid the seed becomes the water that crowns the flower. Nothing from the hard night is wasted. The final words are made of what the winter left behind.  
**Fragments considered and left out:** lines from verse 1 (the painful ideas of love) are shown only as pictures in the water, never as words, so the cold ideas are not given the honor of text. The fragment about the heart learning to dance (in version 1\) is now shown as a dancing light without words, and the idea of learning to live is shown by the figure lifting her light, which keeps the words few and leaves the expression as the words the audience remembers.

# **15\. Ending and the hold after the music**

When the last piano note ends, the picture does not stop. The rose stands on the shore in glorious early spring dawn, gently breathing (petals open and close by one or two percent, very slowly). The water is clear and still, reflecting the mountains, the radiant golden distance behind them and the rose. Warm particles drift upward toward the arch, where star 7 shines among the others. The star is back on the horizon.  
**The word returns.** About 10 seconds into the hold, the Encouragement phrase returns once, small and soft, beneath star 7 in the upper third: "I'll pray (or root) for you. Everything will go well." It holds for about 10 seconds so the audience leaves the song knowing exactly which of Mother's words they saw.  
**Encouragement in everyday life.** Then, one after another, the dew drops on the rose's petals each show a tiny moving scene in their reflection, as if each drop held a memory, all with figures of light, faceless and clothed: a friend squeezing a student's hand before an exam; a coworker leaning over to an overwhelmed colleague's desk; someone sitting beside a neighbor on a hospital bench, hands folded in prayer; a parent kneeling to a child who has lost a game. Each lasts a few seconds and glows violet when the person being encouraged lifts their head. The message: these words belong in every ordinary day, and wherever they reach, peace settles.  
Over about 90 seconds the dawn colors slowly cool back toward evening blue, so that by the end of the hold the scene is nearly the home night lake again, but now with the rose on the shore and a faint pink glow left in the sky. The final 20 seconds are built as a seamless loop so the media operator can hold it as long as needed during applause, speeches or a slower live performance. Nothing goes black.  
In the last seconds of the hold the home star drifts to rest on the horizon at the left, and one petal comes loose from the rose and floats up out of frame. It carries into song 3, where it lands mid-lake.
